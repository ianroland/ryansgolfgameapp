Scene 1: "Fifty From Three Feet" (Act I, before The Rejection)

Phase 1 Ryan (he/him). Baba is still head groundskeeper at the muni.

What changed: the "You talked" joke now goes up, so talking costs Ryan a putt. The cap gesture is gone. The ending now lands on "Again." The coffee-can reveal is unchanged.

[The municipal course, 6 a.m. Mist on the practice green. Baba pushes an ancient reel mower in perfect stripes. It squeaks on every second stripe. Ryan waits at the edge of the green with a putter.]

RYAN (V.O.): My father has been up at five every morning for thirty years. He says sleep is for people with bad greens.

BABA: You're on my stripes.

[Ryan steps off. Baba finishes the stripe. Squeak.]

RYAN: Baba. I want to turn pro.

[Baba keeps mowing. One full stripe. Squeak. At the end he turns the mower round, checks his watch, and looks at Ryan for the first time.]

BABA: You three-putted the fourth twice this week.

RYAN: The fourth has a ridge.

BABA: I built the ridge. The ridge is fair.

RYAN: There's a qualifier for the Pinnacle Tour next month. It costs money. I'll pay for it myself.

BABA: With what?

RYAN: …Optimism.

BABA: Optimism is not legal tender. I looked it up.

[Baba walks to the maintenance shed, and Ryan follows. Inside are rakes, a reel-mower blade and, on a high shelf, a dented coffee can with masking tape across the front. On the tape, in marker: RYAN — PRO.]

RYAN: …How long has that been there?

BABA: Since you were nine. You said "pro" at nine. I wrote it down.

RYAN: You never said anything.

BABA: You were nine. Nine-year-olds also say "astronaut".

[He doesn't hand Ryan the can. He puts it back on the shelf.]

BABA: This is for the day you need it. Not the day you want it.

RYAN: How will I know the difference?

BABA: You won't. I will.

RYAN: So you're okay with it?

BABA: Ryan, I don't understand half of what you're doing. A man your age should have a job with a parking space. (Beat.) But if you're going to do it, you better win.

[He checks his watch.]

BABA: Fifty putts from three feet. Miss one, we start again from zero.

RYAN: That's—

BABA: Fifty-one. Talking is a putt.

[Ryan opens his mouth. He closes it again.]

BABA: Good. Fifty-one.

Choice:

• "Fifty-one. Fine." Ryan starts putting. (Effects TBD: Baba ♥.)

• "Make it a hundred." Baba stops the mower. He checks his watch, then looks at Ryan as if he's seeing him for the first time. "A hundred and one." (Effects TBD: Baba ♥♥.)

• "Can I have a BOBO MILKY first?" Baba takes a carton from the shed fridge, frees the straw in one move and hands it over. "Fifty-two." (This introduces BOBO MILKY. Effects TBD: Baba ♥.)

[Ryan putts. Baba goes back to mowing, and the mower squeaks once for every putt that drops. Near the end, one lips out. The squeaking stops. Baba, without turning round:]

BABA: Again.

RYAN (V.O.): We were there until nine. He never once said "good". He said "again" a hundred and twelve times. I counted. So did he.

Plants:

• the coffee can, which pays off at registration in The Rejection once the patch below is approved;

• the reel mower ("Buy the mower").

Scene 2: "Not For The Public" (Act III or IV)

Phase 3 Ryan (she/her). A tour-stop practice green, mid-morning.

What changed: Biff's solid-gold divot repair tool is now the scene's big visual payoff. It bends, folds, and is followed by a gold pitchfork; then a broken wooden tee does the job. The tee becomes a relic in a velvet case. Everything else is unchanged.

[Ryan putts. Baba stands behind her, arms crossed, beside a small cooler. Written on its lid in marker: NOT FOR THE PUBLIC.]

BABA: Short.

RYAN: It went in.

BABA: It was short, and then it was lucky. Lucky is short with good manners.

[Biff Fairway III arrives. Behind him walks an assistant carrying two velvet cases, one large and one larger.]

BIFF [confident]: Ryan. Aurum-X. Four hundred dollars a vial. Gold flakes for focus, ginseng for vision, and a second layer of gold flakes in case the first ones fall out.

[He drinks it. A gold flake sticks to his front tooth. It stays there for the rest of the scene.]

[Baba opens the cooler. On the ice inside are small cartons of BOBO MILKY. He hands one to Ryan without looking at her.]

BABA: Drink.

BIFF [laughing]: Is that a juice box? Champions don't drink juice boxes, Ryan. Then again, your putting stroke is very… municipal.

[Baba turns. Slowly. The way a man turns after thirty years of watching people walk across his greens in metal spikes.]

BABA: You don't repair your ball marks.

BIFF: …I'm sorry?

BABA: Hole six. Hole eleven. Two on fourteen. (He points at a deep pit in the practice green, by Biff's left shoe.) And that one. From this morning.

BIFF [worried]: Who ARE you?

BABA: Her father. Her greenkeeper, when she needs one. And today, your problem.

BIFF [confident]: Fine. (He snaps his fingers.) The tool.

The gold repair tool

[The assistant opens the large velvet case. On a cushion inside lies a solid-gold divot repair tool, engraved B.F. III, with a single diamond set in the handle. SFX: one soft, angelic "aaah".]

[Biff kneels and pushes it into the ball mark. Gold is soft. Both prongs bend sideways. SFX: a small, sad "bwoing". He pushes harder, and they bend into a U. He stands on it, and it folds flat into the green. The ball mark is now twice the size, with a gold tool lying in it.]

[Hold.]

[Baba checks his watch.]

BIFF [worried]: …The larger case.

[The assistant opens the larger case. Inside: a gold-plated pitchfork. Biff raises it over the green with both hands.]

BABA: No.

[Biff slowly lowers the pitchfork. Without looking, Baba takes a broken wooden tee from his pocket and holds it out. Biff kneels, uses the tee, and repairs the ball mark perfectly in four small pushes. He stares at it.]

[Long hold.]

BIFF [surprised]: …It's beautiful.

BABA: It's a tee.

[Biff places the broken tee on the velvet cushion. The assistant closes the case reverently. The two of them walk off towards the sixth hole, carrying it between them like a relic.]

The carton

[Halfway there, Biff stops and comes back alone.]

BIFF [confident]: What's actually in that carton? I'll pay ten thousand dollars.

BABA: Not for sale.

BIFF: Twenty.

BABA: Not to a man with four ball marks still on his conscience.

[Biff leaves for good. All this time, Ryan has been trying to get the tiny straw out of its wrapper. Baba takes the carton, frees the straw in one move, pokes it in and hands it back.]

BABA: Thirty years I do this.

[A one-second memory flash: young Ryan at the muni, and Baba handing over the same kind of carton, straw already in.]

RYAN: You stuck up for my putting stroke.

BABA: I said nothing about your putting stroke. Your putting stroke is a disaster. (Beat.) Only I get to say that.

RYAN: Thanks, Baba.

BABA: Fifty from three feet. Then you can have the second one.

[On the carton is a strip of masking tape in Baba's handwriting: REPAIR YOUR BALL MARKS. — B.]

Timing:

• One big gag: the gold tool. Its fold, the pitchfork and the tee are its escalation.

• Pauses: "It's a tee.", then "Only I get to say that."

• The gold flake on Biff's tooth is a background gag. It never lands on a punchline.

Later payoff (flavour only): on Sunday, Biff's assistant carries the velvet case with the broken tee in it.

Scene 3: "Reading the Green" (the night before Sunday at the Championship)

Phase 3 Ryan (she/her). This plays before encounter 31 and changes nothing in it.

What changed:

• Green tip removed. "Everything breaks to the lake" is gone, and Ryan no longer writes anything down. Baba's green-reading gives no information the player can use.

• Two new comic beats. In the first, a security guard is waved off by an expired badge; in the second, the sprinklers come on at eleven and Baba already has an umbrella. The umbrella is the hinge into the quiet part.

• Kept word for word: the father-daughter dialogue, the choices and the ending.

[The championship course, 10:55 p.m., the night before the final round. Crickets. A half moon over the lake. On the 18th green, a small circle of torchlight moves very slowly. Baba is lying flat on his stomach, one cheek almost on the grass, reading the grain with the flat of his hand.]

RYAN: Baba. They'll arrest you.

BABA: For what? Caring about grass?

RYAN: For lying on the eighteenth green at five to eleven at night.

BABA: (not looking up) Somebody here cuts with a dull blade. I can hear it.

RYAN: You can hear grass?

BABA: I can hear disrespect.

[A second, much bigger torch beam swings across the green. A SECURITY GUARD with a radio.]

GUARD: Sir. The course is closed.

[Without looking up, Baba holds up a laminated badge on a lanyard: HEAD GROUNDSKEEPER, with the muni's name. It's faded, and long expired. The guard reads it for a long time. He reads it again. He nods slowly, switches off his radio and walks away.]

RYAN: That badge is from a different state.

BABA: Grass doesn't know that.

RYAN: And you're retired.

BABA: Nobody told the grass.

[Somewhere, a timer clicks. At exactly eleven o'clock, every sprinkler on the 18th comes on at once. SFX: a long, rising HISSSS. Without looking up, Baba opens an umbrella. He is completely dry. Ryan is completely soaked.]

[Hold.]

[Baba moves the umbrella over her, and lets the sprinklers soak his own shoulder instead.]

BABA: Eleven o'clock. Every course. They're all the same.

[They sit on the fringe under the one umbrella. The sprinklers tick round in a circle; then they stop, and the crickets come back. SFX: knee cracks, two.]

BABA: Tomorrow there are cameras. The Commissioner. The Fairway boy with his gold vitamins. (Beat.) And whatever else it is you're carrying.

RYAN: You know about that?

BABA: I know you. You've held your shoulders like that since you were six and broke the clubhouse window.

RYAN: I didn't—

BABA: I paid for the window, Ryan.

[Beat.]

BABA: I don't need to know what it is. It's yours. You decide it. I only decide breakfast.

[He switches off the torch. There's only the moon.]

BABA: I'm going to say something once, and never again, because it's embarrassing.

RYAN: Okay.

BABA: I don't understand all of it. The tour, the rules, the… all of it. I'm sixty-one. I understand grass. (Beat.) But you were my kid when you were small and loud. You're my kid now. You're my daughter. That's not something you win. That's something you have.

Choice:

• Lean on his shoulder. She says nothing. He lets her. (Effects TBD: Baba ♥♥.)

• "Will you be there tomorrow?" BABA: "Where else would I be? Somebody has to watch the Fairway boy's ball marks." (Effects TBD: Baba ♥.)

• Tell him what she's carrying. BABA: (a long breath) "Okay." (Beat.) "Still breakfast at six." (Per your decision, this changes no knowledge, no Heat and no caper clues. It unlocks only this reply.)

Every choice continues:

BABA: (Beat.) Also, you're going to win.

RYAN: (laughing) There it is.

BABA: I don't understand half of what you're doing. But if you're going to do it, you better win. (Beat.) And if you don't, you come home, I make breakfast, and we go to the range at six. Same as always.

[He takes a carton of BOBO MILKY from his jacket pocket, frees the straw in one move and hands it to her.]

BABA: For tomorrow. Not tonight. Tonight you sleep.

RYAN: You first.

BABA: I can't. The grass is growing.

[He switches the torch back on and goes back to the green. Ryan walks to the clubhouse. Behind her, the small light moves across the 18th, very carefully, all night.]

RYAN (V.O.): He never said it again. He didn't have to.

Timing: the scene opens at 10:55 p.m., so the sprinklers come on at exactly eleven. There are two big gags, the badge and the sprinklers, with four lines between them. After the umbrella moves over Ryan, there are no gags and no background loops until "Also, you're going to win."
