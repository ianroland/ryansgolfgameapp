Summary
• Baba becomes Ryan's primary family relationship and emotional anchor. He's blunt, demanding, funny and fiercely loyal, and he shows love through practical things rather than speeches. He doesn't fully understand Ryan's transition or the women's tour, and he never stops being her dad.
• Mom stays in the story but at a distance. No reason is given for it, and she's never a villain.
• The coffee can passes to Baba. This is a proposed amendment to the locked Rejection; the comedy stays as it is.
• BOBO MILKY is proposed as a consumable that steadies Ryan under pressure. It's tied to Baba and played against Biff's absurd supplements.
• The three Baba scenes you asked for are drafted below, along with every scene that would need a family-continuity change.
Family guardrails
1.  Baba's love never depends on Ryan winning or on who she is. He demands winning the way some dads demand homework, but every scene makes clear that losing changes nothing about the love.
2.  He doesn't always understand, and he never withholds respect. He uses Ryan's name and, in Phase 3, she/her. His confusion is about the situation (the tour, the rules, the secrecy), never about whether she deserves love. Misgendering is never a joke.
3.  Ryan's choices are hers. Baba criticizes her putting, never her identity. He never decides for her, including about filing or disclosure.
4.  He isn't a caricature. His English is written straight, with no phonetic accent. Cultural detail is specific and light: BOBO MILKY, and the occasional habibi or habibti. His jokes come from his character (a groundskeeper's eye, a coach's bluntness), never from his background.
5.  Mom isn't a villain. She's distant, for reasons the story doesn't explain. She's never cruel, never mocked, and never contrasted unkindly with Baba.
Baba's recurring philosophy: "Ryan, I don't understand half of what you're doing. But if you're going to do it, you better win."
Baba: character proposal
Who he is:
• Ryan's father, Lebanese-American, about 60.
• For thirty years he was head groundskeeper at the municipal course where Ryan learned golf.
• He gave Ryan the hand-me-down driver that beat Biff's gold one in Act I. That's already in the Dossier ("You beat my gold driver with a hand-me-down"), so this only gives it an owner.
• His first name is yours to choose; this proposal uses only "Baba".
Personality:
• Blunt and demanding. He's Ryan's harshest critic: "Short putts never go in. I've told you since you were six."
• Funny, never sentimental. He uses jokes and practical help where other people make speeches.
• Fiercely loyal. The moment an outsider attacks Ryan, the critic becomes a wall.
• Measures success by dedication, discipline and winning, and judges everyone by whether they repair their ball marks.
• Shows affection through practical gestures: BOBO MILKY, the coffee can, the range at six, reading a green for her with a flashlight the night before a final.
• Sometimes struggles to understand Ryan's choices and says so plainly. Then he helps anyway.
• Has his own life and agency. For 31 years he's been chasing a hole-in-one on the muni's 7th hole, and he logs every attempt in a notebook labelled ATTEMPTS. He is not a supporting prop.
Running gag, his groundskeeper's eye: Baba counts other people's unrepaired ball marks. Biff never repairs his. Baba always knows exactly how many. A second tic, from the art: he checks his gold watch whenever anyone is wasting his time, which is usually Biff.
Knowledge (no system change): Baba is Ryan's father, so he knows her past. He joins the inner circle, which scores 0 Heat and never tells. He has no accidental-leak rule. Mom's menu slip stays with Mom (see the next section). Whether he knows about the filing is up to Ryan; none of the scenes below depend on it.

BOBO MILKY: concept, product check, licensing
Concept:
• BOBO MILKY is the drink Baba bought Ryan after every junior round, win or lose.
• On tour, a carton still steadies her.
• It's cheap, it's from home, and it has nothing to do with performance science. That makes it the opposite of Biff's absurd supplements.
• It's treated with affection and is never the butt of a joke.
Product check: please confirm before anything goes further.
• I couldn't verify a Lebanese drink sold as "BOBO MILKY". Searches turned up no product, maker or packaging under that name.
• The closest match is a different company in the same category. Bobofoods & Beverages Limited, a Nigerian company, sells Bobo-branded fruit milk drinks. Other businesses also use "Bobo" for drinks.
• Please tell me the exact product, its maker and where it's sold. If it's a family name or a regional nickname rather than a commercial brand, that changes the licensing picture completely.
Branding and licensing considerations (not legal advice):
• A real product's name, logo and packaging look are normally protected as trademarks and trade dress. Using them in a commercial game without permission risks an infringement claim, or a claim that the maker endorsed the game.
• Because "Bobo" is already used on milk drinks elsewhere, even an invented BOBO MILKY could sit close to an existing mark. A trademark search or a short legal review is worth doing before implementation.
• The options, in order of safety:
    1.  An original in-game name and packaging inspired by the real drink.
    2.  A licence or written permission from the maker, if it's a real brand you want to name.
    3.  The real name with original packaging, only after legal advice.
• Whichever route: no copied logos or packaging, and no real-world claims that the drink improves performance, health or focus. The in-game effect is framed as comfort and confidence from home, not nutrition.
The draft uses BOBO MILKY as a working name throughout, so it's easy to swap later.

Scene 1: "Fifty From Three Feet" (Act I, before The Rejection)
Phase 1 Ryan (he/him). Ryan's first conversation with Baba about going pro.
[The municipal course, 6 a.m. Mist on the practice green. Baba pushes an ancient reel mower in perfect stripes. It squeaks on every second stripe. Ryan waits at the edge of the green with a putter.]
RYAN (V.O.): My father has been up at five every morning for thirty years. He says sleep is for people with bad greens.
BABA: You're on my stripes.
[Ryan steps off. Baba finishes the stripe. Squeak.]
RYAN: Baba. I want to turn pro.
[Baba keeps mowing. One full stripe. Squeak. At the end he turns the mower round and looks at Ryan for the first time.]
BABA: You three-putted the fourth twice this week.
RYAN: The fourth has a ridge.
BABA: I built the ridge. The ridge is fair.
RYAN: There's a qualifier for the Pinnacle Tour next month. It costs money. I'll pay for it myself.
BABA: With what?
RYAN: …Optimism.
BABA: Optimism is not legal tender. I looked it up.
[Baba walks to the maintenance shed, and Ryan follows. Inside are rakes, a reel-mower blade, and, on a high shelf, a dented coffee can with masking tape across the front. On the tape, in marker: RYAN — PRO.]
RYAN: …How long has that been there?
BABA: Since you were nine. You said "pro" at nine. I wrote it down.
RYAN: You never said anything.
BABA: You were nine. Nine-year-olds also say "astronaut".
[He doesn't hand Ryan the can. He puts it back on the shelf.]
BABA: This is for the day you need it. Not the day you want it.
RYAN: How will I know the difference?
BABA: You won't. I will.
RYAN: So you're okay with it?
BABA: Ryan, I don't understand half of what you're doing. A man your age should have a job with a parking space. (Beat.) But if you're going to do it, you better win.
[He checks his gold watch.]
BABA: Fifty putts from three feet. Miss one, we start again.
RYAN: That's—
BABA: Forty-nine. You talked.
Choice:
• "Fifty. Fine." Ryan just starts. (Effects TBD: Baba ♥, a small putting-practice gain.)
• "Make it a hundred." Baba stops mowing. He doesn't smile, but he takes his cap off and puts it back on. (Effects TBD: Baba ♥♥, an Energy cost.)
• "Can I have a BOBO MILKY first?" Baba takes one from the shed fridge, frees the straw in one move and hands it over: "Fifty-one." (Introduces BOBO MILKY. Effects TBD: Baba ♥.)
[Ryan putts. Baba goes back to mowing. The mower squeaks in time with every putt that drops. When Ryan misses the forty-eighth, the squeaking stops. Baba, without turning round:]
BABA: Fifty.
RYAN (V.O.): We were there until nine. He never once said "good". He said "again" a hundred and twelve times. I counted. So did he.
Plants: the coffee can, which pays off at registration in The Rejection if you approve the amendment, and the reel mower ("Buy the mower").
Scene 2: "Not For The Public" (Act III or IV: the BOBO MILKY encounter)
Phase 3 Ryan (she/her). A tour-stop practice green.
[Ryan putts. Baba stands behind her, arms crossed, beside a small cooler. Written on its lid in marker: NOT FOR THE PUBLIC.]
BABA: Short.
RYAN: It went in.
BABA: It was short, and then it was lucky. Lucky is short with good manners.
[Biff Fairway III arrives with an assistant carrying a velvet case. Inside, on a cushion, is a single gold vial.]
BIFF [confident]: Ryan. Aurum-X. Four hundred dollars a vial. Gold flakes for focus, ginseng for vision, and a second layer of gold flakes in case the first ones fall out.
[He drinks it. A gold flake sticks to his front tooth. It stays there for the rest of the scene.]
[Baba opens the cooler. On the ice inside are small cartons of BOBO MILKY. He hands one to Ryan without looking at her.]
BABA: Drink.
BIFF [laughing]: Is that a juice box? Champions don't drink juice boxes, Ryan. Then again, your putting stroke is very… municipal.
[Baba turns. Slowly. The way a man turns after thirty years of watching people walk across his greens in metal spikes.]
BABA: You don't repair your ball marks.
BIFF: …I'm sorry?
BABA: Hole six. Hole eleven. Two on fourteen. I walked behind you. I count.
BIFF [worried]: Who ARE you?
BABA: Her father. Her greenkeeper, when she needs one. And today, your problem.
BIFF [worried]: …I'll fix them.
BABA: Now.
[Biff walks off towards the sixth with a repair tool, still holding his gold vial. His assistant follows with the empty velvet case. Halfway there, Biff stops and comes back.]
BIFF [confident]: What's actually in that carton? I'll pay ten thousand dollars.
BABA: Not for sale.
BIFF: Twenty.
BABA: Not to a man with four ball marks on his conscience.
[Biff leaves for good. All this time, Ryan has been trying to get the tiny straw out of its wrapper. Baba takes the carton, frees the straw in one move, pokes it in and hands it back.]
BABA: Thirty years I do this.
[A one-second memory flash: young Ryan at the muni, and Baba handing over the same kind of carton, straw already in.]
RYAN: You stuck up for my putting stroke.
BABA: I said nothing about your putting stroke. Your putting stroke is a disaster. (Beat.) Only I get to say that.
RYAN: Thanks, Baba.
BABA: Fifty from three feet. Then you can have the second one.
[On the carton is a strip of masking tape in Baba's handwriting: REPAIR YOUR BALL MARKS. — B.]

Scene 3: "Reading the Green" (the night before Sunday at the Championship)
Phase 3 Ryan (she/her). This plays before encounter 31 and changes nothing in it.
[The championship course, the night before the final round. The 18th green under a half moon. A small circle of torchlight moves slowly across it. Baba is on his knees, reading the grain with the flat of his hand.]
RYAN: Baba. They'll arrest you.
BABA: For what? Caring about grass?
RYAN: For being on the eighteenth green at eleven at night.
BABA: (not looking up) It grows towards the lake. After four o'clock, everything breaks to the lake. Write that down.
[Ryan writes it on the back of her hand. She sits on the fringe. Baba works a while longer, then sits down next to her. SFX: two knee cracks.]
BABA: Tomorrow there are cameras. The Commissioner. The Fairway boy with his gold vitamins. (Beat.) And whatever else it is you're carrying.
RYAN: You know about that?
BABA: I know you. You've held your shoulders like that since you were six and broke the clubhouse window.
RYAN: I didn't—
BABA: I paid for the window, Ryan.
[Beat.]
BABA: I don't need to know what it is. It's yours. You decide it. I only decide breakfast.
[He switches off the torch. There's only the moon.]
BABA: I'm going to say something once, and never again, because it's embarrassing.
RYAN: Okay.
BABA: I don't understand all of it. The tour, the rules, the… all of it. I'm sixty-one. I understand grass. (Beat.) But you were my kid when you were small and loud. You're my kid now. You're my daughter. That's not something you win. That's something you have.

• Tell him what she's carrying. BABA: (a long breath) "Okay." (Beat.) "Still breakfast at six." (Baba is inner circle, so this changes no Heat. Whether it should mark anything is your call. See Decisions, item 5.)
Every choice continues:
BABA: (Beat.) Also, you're going to win.
RYAN: (laughing) There it is.
BABA: I don't understand half of what you're doing. But if you're going to do it, you better win. (Beat.) And if you don't, you come home, I make breakfast, and we go to the range at six. Same as always.
[He takes a carton of BOBO MILKY from his jacket pocket, frees the straw in one move and hands it to her.]
BABA: For tomorrow. Not tonight. Tonight you sleep.
RYAN: You first.
BABA: I can't. The grass is growing.
[He switches the torch back on and goes back to the green. Ryan walks to the clubhouse. Behind her, the small light moves across the 18th, very carefully, all night.]
RYAN (V.O.): He never said it again. He didn't have to.
